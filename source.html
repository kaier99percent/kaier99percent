05

Light and optics

The section where I have to eat some humble pie.

the oil thing

Main paper — methods section

Science Advances, 2024

They used oil to match the wax's refractive index, so the wax "disappears" from the light's point of view. They used chloroform to actually dissolve it. I mixed those two up — see section 08.

read the paper

Main paper — optics section

Supplementary

The scattering comes from randomly arranged particles. Not a neat pattern like the Morpho butterfly, not a thin film like a soap bubble. Disordered scattering.

Birds

The "berry is basically a delivery service for the plant" section.

Ultraviolet reflection of berries attracts foraging birds: redwings and bilberries

Proc. Royal Society B, 1999

The closest thing to a real test of the "birds like the UV ones" idea. Adult redwings preferred UV-reflecting bilberries when UV light was on. Caveats: bilberries, not blueberries, and young birds didn't show the preference.

08

If I could re-record it

After the video went up, I went back through the papers. Here's what I'd say differently.

no notes

The wax is doing the color work

This holds up. It's literally what the paper says.

too strong

"Blueberries contain no blue pigment"

They do have pigments. They just aren't what makes the outside look blue. I should have said the matte-blue look isn't from a pigment.

what I'd say now "The intense matte-blue look isn't from a blue pigment. It's the wax coating interacting with light."
actually wrong

"The oil dissolves the wax"

This one's just wrong. The oil matched the wax's refractive index, which made the wax invisible to light. Chloroform was used to actually dissolve the wax. Two different experiments and I mashed them together.

correct version "The oil doesn't dissolve the wax. It has a similar refractive index, which stops the light from scattering off the wax the way it normally would."
could be clearer

"The wax scatters blue and UV"

True, but I made it sound like the butterfly thing. It's not. The scattering comes from randomly arranged particles, not a neat ordered pattern.

hypothesis, not fact

"Birds are the target audience"

I said this like it was settled. It's not. The redwing study (section 06) is decent evidence, but it used bilberries and young birds didn't react. Plausible, not proven.

detail I missed

Wax structures aren't all the same

Rings, rods, slabs, tubes. Lots of shapes. I described them like they were one neat structure. The disorder is the whole point of the paper and I undersold it.
